01 / THE STORY

My eyesight may be limited.
My outlook is not.

My visual impairment is part of my story, not the definition of my future.

Growing up with a visual impairment has shaped how I approach life. My upbringing taught me to try new things, take opportunities and focus on what is possible rather than what is not.

I am proud of the independent and fulfilling life I have built. Studying Business Administration at Erasmus University Rotterdam, spending a semester abroad at WU Vienna and taking on leadership responsibilities have given me opportunities to put that mindset into practice.

People are equally important to my identity. I enjoy understanding how others feel, supporting them and creating a positive atmosphere. I want to be someone who is fun to be around, but also someone people can rely on when it matters.

Points of difference

A perspective shaped by visual impairment · emotional intelligence · a possibility-driven mindset · the combination of empathy and decisiveness.

06 / SELF-REFLECTION

The gap between
potential and performance.

One of my strengths is adapting and making decisions in the moment. But spontaneity can become a weakness when I fail to plan ahead.

Academic motivation is another development area. Because I often achieve satisfactory results without investing significant time, I have not always developed the discipline needed to reach my full potential. I am much more motivated by things that genuinely interest me.

There is a tension between my values and my own behaviour: I expect commitment from colleagues, yet I do not always demonstrate that same commitment in my academic life.

To become the leader I aspire to be, I need to combine my natural strengths with greater structure and consistency.

What I bring

Emotional intelligence · public speaking · positivity · decisiveness · willingness to try new things.

What I need to build

Planning · academic motivation · consistency · discipline in less interesting tasks.

My proposed next steps

Set three weekly priorities, schedule focused study blocks and review my progress each Sunday. Ask for feedback on how I lead and communicate.

My ambition is not to change who I am, but to become a more consistent version of myself.

Brand name & monogram

My own name makes the brand personal and accountable. The intertwined M and S represent a single identity, with a contrasting accent suggesting a different perspective.

Brand slogan

“See possibilities. Not limitations.” connects my positive outlook with a subtle reference to visual impairment.

Typography & accessibility

Expressive serif headlines and clear sans-serif body text balance personality and professionalism. Readable text, contrast, keyboard navigation and adjustable text size reflect the importance of accessibility.
